Untold Stories: changes to apply to the Shorts automation
Paste this whole file into the chatbot that writes and posts Untold Stories Shorts. It replaces the old rules wherever they conflict.

Context (why these changes)
Untold Stories (@Untold_Stories844) posts Hindi-narrated Indian mythology Shorts. On 25 Nov 2026 every Short on the channel was compared with every Short on Maya Effects (@maya_effects.official, 300K subscribers, same niche, Hindi narration). Key evidence:

Maya Shorts that open with a contradiction stated as a fact had a median of 784K views (17 Shorts). Slow openers (scene-setting, a shloka, a disclaimer, music) had 84K (7 Shorts). All 7 of their 1M+ Shorts open with a contradiction.
12 of Untold Stories' first 13 titles were "क्यों / कैसे / किसने" questions; only 6 of Maya's 32 titles are questions. Maya's title is the spoken first line.
Ramayana is Maya's best topic (13 Shorts, median 588K). Little-known figures did worst on Untold Stories (Aruna 40 views, Matsya 19, Kaliya 9); famous ones did best (Govardhan/Krishna 747, Shiva's trident 366, Nandi 217).
Maya posts 1 Short a day, mostly 15:00 to 17:15 IST. Untold Stories was posting 3 a day.
Maya's 1M+ Shorts are 80 to 105 seconds long. Untold Stories' were 64 to 74.
YouTube's captions detected music in the first 9 seconds of 5 of 13 Untold Stories Shorts, and in only 1 of Maya's 32 (their worst Short).
Maya's multi-part "Episode" Shorts had a median of 73K versus 181K for standalone Shorts in the same period.
These are patterns from public data, not guarantees.

Rules for every Short
1. Opening line (most important)
The first spoken sentence is a contradiction or surprising fact, stated as a fact, about a figure most Indians already know.
It must work in under 3 seconds: one or two short sentences, ideally 8 to 16 Hindi words.
Do not open with a question ("क्यों", "क्या आप जानते हैं"), scene-setting ("लंका का रणक्षेत्र…"), a shloka, a disclaimer, the channel name or music.
Put "कहा जाता है", source notes and any disclaimer after the hook, never before it.
Hook patterns that worked:
X never did Y. So who did? "14 साल के वनवास में लक्ष्मण एक बार भी नहीं सोए।"
X couldn't do A, yet did B. "श्री राम अपने पिता की चिता को अग्नि नहीं दे पाए। पर एक गिद्ध की चिता उन्होंने अपने हाथों से जलाई।"
Even X couldn't, because of Y. "मृत्यु भी राम तक नहीं पहुँच पा रही थी, क्योंकि उनके द्वार पर हनुमान खड़े थे।"
Two from one, opposite fates. "एक ही माँ के दो बेटे। एक बने विष्णु की शय्या, दूसरे शिव के गले का हार।"
What you believe is wrong. "सोने की लंका कभी रावण की थी ही नहीं।"
2. Title
The title is the same sentence as the first spoken line (or its first half if too long).
Hindi, under about 70 characters.
No "#shorts" and no "| English label" in the title.
On 1 Short in 3, use an English version of the same line as the title (a test). Record which Shorts used English titles.
3. Topic choice
Every Short needs a famous figure plus a fact almost nobody knows.
Lean on the Ramayana (Rama, Sita, Lakshmana, Hanuman, Ravana), then Shiva, Krishna, Vishnu's avatars and the big Mahabharata names (Karna, Bhishma, Arjuna, Draupadi).
Avoid little-known figures as the lead unless the hook names a famous one too.
Every Short must stand alone. No "Episode 1, 2, 3" Shorts.
Festival tie-ins are good when a festival is near (check the date on Drik Panchang).
Don't repeat stories Maya Effects has already posted: Lakshmana's sleep/Urmila, Hanuman at Rama's door/the ring, Sheshnag and Vasuki, Rahu and Ketu, Gajendra, Luv-Kush and Rama's bow, Valmiki's first verse, the Pinaka bow and Sita, Kubera's golden Lanka, Makardhwaj, Ravana as priest at Rameshwaram, Narakasura, Dashavatar as evolution, the Yama lamp on Dhanteras, Chhinnamasta, Garuda and Kakbhushundi, Jatayu, the golden egg, Indra and the ants, the Sati/Daksha story, Gandhari's curse, Rudra refusing to create death, Rama's journey map, Ganga drowning her sons, the Param Vir Chakra's vajra, Ravana's last words to Lakshmana, the giant whose spike made Hanuman bleed.
Stories already used by Untold Stories: Dev Deepawali/Tripura, Aruna, Vishnu's eye, Govardhan's curse, Shiva's trident from the Sun, Matsya, Hanuman's name, Ravana's name, Nandi, Kartikeya's six faces, Barbarik/Khatu Shyam, Krishna and Sudama, Kaliya. Week 1 (25 Nov to 1 Dec): Ahalya, Narasimha, Lakshman Rekha, Karna's armour, Shabari, Savitri, Ram Setu/Nala.
Unused ideas to draw on next: Bhishma choosing his death day, Abhimanyu and the chakravyuha, Arjuna as Brihannala, Markandeya, Neelkanth (Shiva holding the poison in his throat), Ganesha writing the Mahabharata with a broken tusk, Vamana and Bali, Hanuman burning Lanka.

3a. Story style: dark, scary, suspense to the end (user, 25 Nov 2026: "stories are getting boring")
Default for every Short from now on. No more calm "why does X have Y" trivia.
The hook is SCARY: an eerie fact stated as a fact (a curse that still walks, a voice at night, a body that would not die, death waiting at a door, a cremation ground). It still names a famous figure.
Hold back the answer. Keep the viewer asking "phir kya hua? / ye kaun hai? / kyon?" the whole way. Do not explain the mystery early.
Build tension in 2-3 steps: each step makes it worse (a sign, then a second sign, then the thing itself appears).
The conclusion comes only in the last 10-15 seconds: the reveal that explains the hook, then one closing line that leaves a chill ("और कहते हैं, वो आज भी…").
Tone: horror atmosphere, not gore. Darkness, silence, footsteps, whispers, shadows, eyes in the dark. Violence stays off-screen or as silhouettes (family audience).
Still real mythology only: Mahabharata, Ramayana, Puranas, Upanishads, Vetala Panchavimshati, or clearly labelled folk belief ("कहा जाता है"). No invented ghosts or fake "true events".
Story ideas that fit (not used yet): Ashwatthama, cursed to wander with a wound that never heals (Mahabharata, Sauptika Parva); Parikshit sealed in a tower and the snake hidden in a fruit (Bhagavata 1.18-19 / 12.6); Jarasandha, born in two halves and joined by a demoness (Sabha Parva); Raktabija, every drop of blood becomes a new demon (Devi Mahatmya); Bhairava and Brahma's skull that would not leave his hand (Shiva Purana); Nachiketa waiting three nights at Yama's door (Katha Upanishad); Vikram and Betaal on the cremation-ground tree (Vetala Panchavimshati); Shiva as Bhootnath with the ghosts of the shamshan; Kumbhakarna woken by force (already used, don't repeat); Ravana's Ahiravan taking Rama and Lakshmana to Patal (folk Ramayana, label it); Andhaka, Shiva's blind son (Shiva Purana).
4. Script structure and length
**45 to 55 seconds (user, 25 Nov 2026 23:00: "short crispy honi chahiye, hooks strong, end tak audience rakhe"), about 120-135 Hindi words in 14-17 lines.** Crisp: every line 5-10 words, no setup sentences, no repetition; cut any line that adds no new fact or tension. (This replaces 85-100 s.)
Beats:
Hook (0 to 3 s): the contradiction, told as something scary (section 3a).
Second line (3 to 8 s): raises the stakes or reveals the "who/why" question.
Setup: who, where, what was at stake.
Turn: the event.
Twist: the part most people don't know. Keep the full answer for the last 10-15 seconds (the conclusion).
Closing line that flips the hook back on the viewer. Example: "सबको याद है वो भाई जो कभी नहीं सोया। पर उस पत्नी को कौन याद करता है जो उसके लिए सोती रही?"
Call to action: "ऐसी ही अनकही कहानियों के लिए फॉलो करें Untold Stories।"
Short, plain Hindi sentences. Numbers in Hindi words.
Facts: use the main text (Valmiki, Mahabharata, Puranas, Ramcharitmanas) and name it. If a detail comes from a later retelling or folk tradition, say so after the hook ("कहा जाता है", "तुलसीदास लिखते हैं"). Don't invent dialogue, numbers or scripture.
Family audience: keep violence as silhouettes and sensitive episodes to one line.
5. Sound
The voice starts at 0.0 to 0.3 seconds. No music, sting or sound effect before the first word.
Voice first: music stays well under the voice for the whole Short (a good guide is at least 15 dB below the voice), and the overall loudness is about −14 LUFS.
6. Visuals and on-screen text
Frame 1 shows the contradiction (the mountain shrinking, the stone statue crumbling, the god with no legs), not a calm establishing shot.
Pick one signature art style and use it on every Short. Don't rotate styles. Don't copy Maya Effects' leather-puppet (Tholu Bommalata) look. Choose a different Indian art form, such as Pattachitra, Madhubani, Kalighat, Phad or Kerala mural.
One text layer at a time: the Hinglish caption only. No top title banner plus caption box plus logo badge stacked together.
Each caption is one short phrase, placed in an empty part of the frame.
Keep the existing end card.
7. Description
Use this template:

<the hook line in Hindi>
<the same line in English>

<one or two lines saying what happens, without giving away the twist>
<a question for comments, e.g. "क्या आपको ये पहले से पता था? 👇">

Sources:
- <text, book/kanda/parva, chapter or sarga>
- <note any later retelling or folk tradition used>

#<Figure> #<Epic> #IndianMythology #shorts
Use 3 to 5 hashtags, not 8 or more.

8. Posting schedule
1 Short a day at 4:15 PM IST, every day, scheduled in YouTube Studio (Visibility → Schedule, time zone GMT+5:30).
A second Short on the same day only if it is just as strong, at 7:30 PM IST. Never 3 a day.
9. Long videos (start in week 3)
Once a week, make one 6 to 10 minute video that joins 4 to 6 related Shorts around one figure (for example "Krishna's 5 untold stories").
Post a teaser Short the same day that holds back the ending and says the full story is in the long video.
In YouTube Studio, set the "Related video" on each of those Shorts to the long video.
Make playlists by figure: Krishna, Shiva, Hanuman, Ramayana, Mahabharata.
10. Other
Cross-post the same Shorts to Instagram Reels.
If YouTube Studio offers auto-dubbing on the channel, turn it on (a bonus, not a priority). Keep Hindi as the narration language.
